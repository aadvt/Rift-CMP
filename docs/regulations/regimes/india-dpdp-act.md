# India — Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023

## 1. Regulation Identity

- **Official name:** Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023
- **Act number:** Act No. 22 of 2023
- **Jurisdiction:** India
- **Enacted:** 11 August 2023
- **Primary regulator:** Data Protection Board of India
- **Primary subject:** Processing of digital personal data in India.
- **Instrument type:** Central legislation.
- **Implementation:** Provisions come into force on dates notified by the Central Government.

## 2. Primary Sources

- Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 — Government of India / MeitY.
- Digital Personal Data Protection Rules, 2025 — where Rules provide implementation details.
- Notifications issued under the Act.
- Orders and regulations of the Data Protection Board of India.
- Government notifications identifying countries/territories or classes of Data Fiduciaries where applicable.

## 3. Territorial / Applicability Scope

The Act applies to:

1. Processing of digital personal data within India where the personal data is collected:
   - in digital form; or
   - in non-digital form and subsequently digitised.

2. Processing outside India where the processing is in connection with offering goods or services to Data Principals within India.

The Act does not apply to specified personal/domestic processing and other exclusions provided by the Act.

## 4. Covered Actors

### 4.1 Data Principal

The individual to whom the personal data relates.

### 4.2 Data Fiduciary

A person who, alone or in conjunction with other persons, determines the purpose and means of processing personal data.

### 4.3 Data Processor

A person who processes personal data on behalf of a Data Fiduciary.

### 4.4 Consent Manager

A person registered with the Board who enables a Data Principal to give, manage, review and withdraw consent through an accessible and transparent platform.

### 4.5 Significant Data Fiduciary

A Data Fiduciary designated by the Central Government based on factors specified by the Act, including volume and sensitivity of personal data, risk to the sovereignty and integrity of India, risk to electoral democracy, security of the State, and public order.

## 5. Covered Data

The Act regulates **digital personal data**.

### Personal Data

Any data about an individual who is identifiable by or in relation to such data.

### Digital Personal Data

Personal data in digital form, including personal data collected in non-digital form and subsequently digitised.

The Act does not create GDPR-style statutory categories such as "special categories of personal data".

## 6. Lawful Processing

A Data Fiduciary may process personal data only:

- for a lawful purpose; and
- with the consent of the Data Principal or for certain legitimate uses specified by the Act.

The Act therefore should not be represented as a simple consent-only regime.

## 7. Consent Requirements

Consent must be:

- free;
- specific;
- informed;
- unconditional; and
- unambiguous.

Consent must involve a clear affirmative action and be limited to personal data necessary for the specified purpose.

The Data Fiduciary must be able to demonstrate that notice was given and consent was obtained in accordance with the Act.

## 8. Notice

Before or with a request for consent, the Data Fiduciary must provide notice containing information required by the Act.

The notice must communicate:

- the personal data proposed to be processed;
- the purpose of processing; and
- information necessary for exercising rights under the Act.

The Act requires notices and consent requests to be presented in clear and plain language.

## 9. When Consent Is Not the Relevant Authorization

The Act recognises **certain legitimate uses** where processing may occur without consent.

Examples provided by the Act include specified circumstances such as:

- voluntary provision of personal data for a specified purpose;
- performance of certain State functions;
- provision of services or benefits by the State;
- performance of functions under law;
- compliance with legal obligations;
- medical emergencies;
- provision of medical treatment;
- public-health situations;
- disaster response;
- employment-related purposes; and
- other legitimate uses expressly specified by the Act.

Each legitimate use must be mapped to the relevant statutory provision.

## 10. Withdrawal of Consent

A Data Principal may withdraw consent at any time.

Withdrawal must be as easy as giving consent.

After withdrawal, the Data Fiduciary must cease processing unless processing is otherwise authorised by the Act or another applicable law.

Where a Data Processor is involved, the Data Fiduciary must ensure corresponding cessation of processing where required.

## 11. Data Principal Rights

The Act provides rights including:

### Right to Access Information

A Data Principal may obtain information about personal data and processing as provided by the Act.

### Right to Correction and Erasure

A Data Principal may request correction, completion, updating or erasure of personal data as provided by the Act.

### Right to Grievance Redressal

A Data Principal has a right to grievance redressal.

### Right to Nominate

A Data Principal may nominate another individual to exercise specified rights in the event of death or incapacity.

## 12. Duties of Data Principals

Data Principals also have statutory duties, including:

- complying with applicable law;
- not impersonating another person;
- not suppressing material information when required by law;
- providing authentic information where required for specified purposes; and
- observing applicable duties under the Act.

## 13. General Obligations of Data Fiduciaries

A Data Fiduciary must:

- comply with the Act;
- comply with applicable Rules;
- ensure processing is lawful;
- provide required notices;
- obtain valid consent where required;
- provide mechanisms for Data Principal rights;
- maintain appropriate technical and organisational measures;
- implement reasonable security safeguards;
- notify personal data breaches as required;
- erase personal data when retention is no longer necessary, subject to legal requirements;
- ensure Data Processors comply with applicable requirements; and
- establish grievance redressal mechanisms.

## 14. Data Minimisation / Purpose Limitation

Processing should be connected to the specified purpose for which the personal data is processed.

The Data Fiduciary should not retain personal data beyond the period necessary for the specified purpose unless retention is required or authorised by law.

## 15. Security Safeguards

Data Fiduciaries must implement reasonable security safeguards to prevent personal data breaches.

The safeguards must be appropriate to the risks involved.

The Rules provide additional requirements concerning security safeguards.

## 16. Personal Data Breach

A Data Fiduciary must notify:

- the Data Protection Board of India; and
- affected Data Principals,

in accordance with the Act and applicable Rules.

The detailed form, content and timing of notifications are addressed by the Rules.

## 17. Children

A **child** is an individual who has not completed 18 years of age.

Before processing children's personal data, a Data Fiduciary must obtain verifiable consent of the parent or lawful guardian as required by the Act.

A Data Fiduciary must not undertake processing that is likely to cause a detrimental effect on the well-being of a child.

The Act also restricts:

- tracking or behavioural monitoring of children; and
- targeted advertising directed at children,

subject to statutory exceptions and exemptions.

## 18. Significant Data Fiduciaries

A Significant Data Fiduciary has additional obligations.

These include:

- appointing a Data Protection Officer;
- appointing an independent data auditor;
- undertaking periodic Data Protection Impact Assessments;
- undertaking periodic audits; and
- complying with additional obligations prescribed by the Rules.

## 19. Data Protection Officer

A Significant Data Fiduciary must appoint a Data Protection Officer who:

- represents the Significant Data Fiduciary;
- is responsible for compliance-related functions;
- acts as the point of contact for Data Principals;
- acts as the point of contact for the Data Protection Board; and
- performs other prescribed responsibilities.

## 20. Data Processor / Vendor Requirements

A Data Fiduciary may engage a Data Processor to process personal data on its behalf.

The Data Fiduciary remains responsible for complying with the Act for processing carried out on its behalf.

Contracts and operational controls should define:

- processing purpose;
- scope;
- security obligations;
- breach handling;
- deletion/return;
- sub-processing where applicable; and
- audit/compliance responsibilities.

## 21. International Transfers

The Act permits the Central Government to restrict transfer of personal data by notification to specified countries or territories outside India.

Therefore:

- international transfer is not represented as universally prohibited;
- destination restrictions must be tracked separately;
- current government notifications must be checked before implementing jurisdiction-specific transfer rules.

## 22. Retention and Erasure

The Data Fiduciary must erase personal data when:

- the purpose for which it was collected is no longer served; and
- retention is not necessary for a legal purpose.

The Act and Rules contain additional requirements for specified Data Fiduciaries and circumstances.

Retention must therefore be represented as:

`purpose + retention condition + legal requirement + applicable Rule`

rather than as one universal retention period.

## 23. Sale / Sharing / Targeted Advertising

The DPDP Act does not use the CCPA's "sale" and "sharing" framework.

The product should therefore not translate DPDP processing automatically into:

- `sale = true`
- `sharing = true`

Instead, model the actual processing purpose, Data Fiduciary/Data Processor relationship, consent or legitimate-use basis, and applicable obligations.

Children's targeted advertising and behavioural monitoring have specific statutory restrictions.

## 24. Grievance Redressal

Data Fiduciaries must establish an effective mechanism for Data Principals to exercise grievance redressal rights.

A Data Principal generally must exhaust the Data Fiduciary's grievance mechanism before approaching the Board, subject to the statutory framework.

## 25. Data Protection Board of India

The Act establishes the Data Protection Board of India.

The Board has functions and powers including:

- receiving complaints;
- inquiring into breaches;
- directing remedial measures;
- imposing penalties; and
- performing other statutory functions.

## 26. Penalties / Enforcement

The Act provides financial penalties for specified breaches.

The maximum penalty under the Schedule can reach **₹250 crore** for a breach of the obligation to take reasonable security safeguards to prevent personal data breach.

Different contraventions have different maximum penalties.

The product must therefore model:

`violation type -> statutory provision -> applicable penalty`

rather than one generic penalty value.

## 27. Exemptions

The Act contains exemptions and special provisions for specified circumstances, including certain:

- State functions;
- legal/judicial functions;
- law-enforcement purposes;
- research/archiving/statistical purposes;
- specified processing by notified classes of entities; and
- other circumstances expressly provided by the Act.

Exemptions must be represented as conditions rather than universal exclusions.

## 28. Effective Dates / Versioning

The Act received Presidential assent on:

**11 August 2023**

The Central Government notified phased commencement on **13 November 2025**.

The commencement notification provides:

- selected institutional and administrative provisions effective on publication;
- certain provisions effective one year after publication;
- the principal operational provisions effective eighteen months after publication.

Therefore, as of the research date, the DPDP Act must be represented with **provision-level effective dates** rather than one global `effective_date`.

## 30. Ambiguities / Open Questions

- Application of the Act to complex cross-border processing.
- Interpretation of specific legitimate-use categories.
- Treatment of emerging tracking technologies.
- Interaction with sector-specific Indian legislation.
- Government notifications restricting international transfers.
- Detailed implementation of exemptions.
- Future Board regulations, orders and guidance.

## 31. Conflicts Between Sources

If the Act, Rules, notification, Board order, or regulator guidance appear to differ:

1. Record each source separately.
2. Record publication date.
3. Record effective date.
4. Identify the exact provision.
5. Do not silently resolve the conflict.
6. Escalate unresolved legal interpretation as an open question.

## 32. Research Notes

- DPDP uses **Data Principal** and **Data Fiduciary** terminology.
- DPDP does not use GDPR's controller/processor terminology as its primary statutory terminology.
- DPDP does not establish GDPR-style special-category personal data.
- DPDP has a broad child threshold of under 18.
- Consent is only one authorization path.
- Legitimate uses must be represented separately from consent.
- DPDP does not use the CCPA concepts of sale and sharing.
- The Act and Rules must be versioned independently.
- Commencement is phased and must be represented at provision level.
